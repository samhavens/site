# source and assumption ledger

Research date: September 24, 2026. Dates below are the actual source/survey dates, not claims that the observations describe 2026. The essay includes ordinary footnotes so it can be published outside this chat.

## demographic anchors

| primary source | supported anchor | important limit |
|---|---|---|
| Pew, [size of the US Jewish population](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2021/05/11/the-size-of-the-u-s-jewish-population/), May 2021; survey 2019–2020 | 2.4% of adults under Pew's Jewish definition; includes Jews of no religion | not a biological-ancestry census, all-age model state, or present-day fitted parameter |
| Pew, [marriage, families and children](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2021/05/11/marriage-families-and-children/), May 2021 | branch-specific intermarriage among currently married respondents: Orthodox 2%, Conservative 25%, Reform 42%, unaffiliated 68%; recent-marriage measures have different denominators | intact marriages surviving to survey time, not all reproductive unions or future subgroup rates |
| Pew, [intermarriage and other demographics](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2013/10/01/chapter-2-intermarriage-and-other-demographics/), October 2013, fertility section | children among respondents aged 40–59: Orthodox 4.1, Conservative 1.8, Reform 1.7 | respondent-based completed-fertility approximation; not exact same-group pair fertility or a measured Haredi/modern split |
| Pew, [Jewish demographics](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2021/05/11/jewish-demographics/), May 2021 | fertility measures need the stated age denominator | the all-adult Orthodox/non-Orthodox averages must not be relabeled completed fertility |
| Ausubel, Smith, Cooperman, [denominational switching](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2021/06/22/denominational-switching-among-u-s-jews-reform-judaism-has-gained-conservative-judaism-has-lost/), June 2021 | retrospective upbringing/current affiliation show substantial switching | not a prospective child-to-adult transition matrix; survey ages and denominators matter |
| Pew, [Jewish Americans in 2020](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2021/05/11/jewish-americans-in-2020/), May 2021 | younger adults' Orthodox share differs from the overall adult share; 17% at 18–29 versus 9% overall | demonstrates the relevance of age structure; does not supply the cohort model's initial age distribution |
| Ralph & Coop, [The Geography of Recent Genetic Ancestry across Europe](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1001555), PLOS Biology, 2013 | genetic and genealogical ancestry differ; population structure and geography matter | not an estimate of Jewish ancestors among Americans and not evidence for the previous 8% claim |

All model projections, subgroup-specific future rates, arrival assumptions, clustering, identity transition rows, the 6% Haredi/3% other-Orthodox mixture, and the 8% alternative ancestry starting point are **assumptions or derived outputs**, not findings quoted from those sources.

The pure OR inheritance recurrence and frozen-average-control construction are mathematical definitions/derivations. They require no empirical citation to be valid as conditional calculations; using them to describe an actual country requires independently defended empirical inputs.
